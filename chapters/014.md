# Day 14 — Neither Fear nor Worship the Flesh

> “Don't fear the flesh, nor love it. If you fear it, it'll rule you. If you love it, it'll devour and strangle you.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

We have a complicated relationship with our bodies. We depend upon them for every experience of this world, yet we often treat them as though they were something separate from us. We admire them, criticize them, discipline them, indulge them, compare them, fear what will happen to them, and sometimes wish they would simply behave.

Do not fear the flesh, and do not worship it. The tension matters. We can become so afraid of bodily desire that desire itself begins to feel dangerous. Hunger, pleasure, sexuality, comfort, fatigue, and longing become forces that must constantly be watched, controlled, and subdued. Yet whatever we fear has already gained a certain kind of power over us.

If we spend our lives terrified that an appetite might overwhelm us, much of our attention remains fixed upon the appetite. We monitor it, argue with it, condemn it, and promise ourselves that tomorrow we will finally master it. The struggle itself can become another form of captivity.

But the other direction offers no freedom either. We can make appetite our master by assuming that every desire deserves satisfaction. Hunger appears, so we eat; discomfort appears, so we escape; desire appears, so we follow it. The body speaks, and we obey before asking what the desire is actually asking for. Then what we love can devour us.

There is another way between these extremes. We can listen to the body without fearing it and care for it without worshiping it. We can allow desire to speak without automatically turning desire into command. This requires curiosity.

Hunger may simply mean the body needs food. But sometimes the desire to eat carries something else with it: exhaustion, loneliness, boredom, sadness, celebration, or the longing to be comforted. The appetite is real even when the thing it asks for is not the thing we most deeply need.

The same is true of many desires. We reach for distraction when we need rest. We reach for approval when we need belonging, achievement when we need worth, certainty when we need safety, and pleasure when we need relief. None of these desires makes us bad; they simply reveal that the human creature is always reaching toward something it believes will bring life.

Attention allows us to ask where the desire is pointing. This is very different from shame. Shame sees appetite and immediately begins constructing a verdict about character. Curiosity sees the same appetite and asks what is happening beneath the surface. One condemns; the other listens.

Perhaps this is part of what it means to become free from the flesh without becoming an enemy of the body. Freedom is not the absence of desire. Freedom is the growing ability to experience desire without losing ourselves inside it.

We can feel hunger without becoming hunger. We can feel fear without becoming fear. We can notice attraction, anger, exhaustion, pleasure, or craving without pretending these experiences do not exist and without surrendering the whole self to them. The human inside the human remains larger than any single impulse passing through the body.

This allows the body to become a teacher rather than an enemy. A tightening chest may tell us that something feels unsafe. Fatigue may reveal limits we have refused to respect. Tears may carry grief the mind has been trying to explain away, while a deep breath may tell us that somewhere inside we have finally stopped defending ourselves.

The body speaks a language worth learning. Perhaps this is why neither fear nor worship is enough. Both make the body too powerful. Fear turns it into an adversary that must be defeated, while worship turns it into a ruler whose desires must be obeyed.

Love offers something gentler. We can feed the body because it is worthy of care. We can move it, rest it, enjoy it, listen to it, and sometimes tell it no. A loving parent does not satisfy every request of a child, nor does a loving parent despise the child for asking.

Perhaps we can learn to offer ourselves the same kindness. There will be moments when appetite becomes loud. There will be times when old patterns return and times when we choose something that does not serve the life we are trying to become. None of this requires turning against ourselves.

The pearl remains a pearl even in the mud. Transformation does not require hatred of what is unfinished. The divine dyer works patiently, allowing the color to reach deeper fibers over time. Light enters rooms gradually, revealing what was already there and showing us what still needs healing.

So do not fear the flesh. Do not worship it either. Listen to it with compassion, receive its pleasures with gratitude, respect its limits, and become curious about its hungers. Let the body be part of the journey rather than the obstacle standing in its way.

You are neither a disembodied spirit nor a collection of appetites. You are a living human being learning how to become whole.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** I receive this body.

> **Exhale:** Teach me freedom within it.
