# Day 3 — The One Truth in Many Things

> “There's only one Truth, but it's many things for us, to teach this one thing in love through many things.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

We like things that can be contained. Give something a name, draw a boundary around it, write down its definition, and there is a certain comfort in believing we now possess it. This is especially tempting with the things that matter most. We want to know what God is, what salvation means, what happens after death, what is true and what is false. Uncertainty makes us restless.

But Truth may be larger than the vessels we use to carry it. There is only one Truth, yet it becomes many things for us. Not many competing truths, each fighting for supremacy, but one reality revealing itself through many forms. Light through a window. Bread upon a table. Water poured over a body. A seed disappearing beneath the earth. A father welcoming a child. A bride and bridegroom becoming one. Each reveals something the others cannot.

Consider light. We can speak of God as light because light reveals what was hidden. It warms. It awakens. It allows us to see. Yet light cannot tell us everything about God. Light does not embrace us. So another image is given.

God is Father. Now there is relationship. Origin. Belonging. We are no longer simply standing in illumination; we are being drawn into a family. Yet even *Father* cannot contain the whole. No human relationship, however beautiful, can bear the weight of everything divine.

And so another image arrives. Bread. Now God is not merely the One from whom we come but the One upon whom we live. Bread must be received. It must be taken into us. What was outside becomes part of us, nourishing muscle and blood and breath. Suddenly communion with God is not merely something we understand. It is something we eat.

Then comes water. Then fire. Then Spirit. Then bride and bridegroom. The images keep coming because the mystery keeps exceeding them. Perhaps this is why Truth teaches “in love through many things.” There is gentleness in that phrase. Truth does not demand that we comprehend the infinite all at once. It comes near through things we already know. It meets us in ordinary matter and ordinary relationships and says, *Look here. There is something I want to show you.*

A loaf of bread can become a doorway. So can a cup of wine. So can sunlight falling across the floor. So can the face of someone you love. The mistake would be to stop at the doorway.

We can become so attached to one image that we begin defending it against all the others. We discover something true about God and quietly turn that discovery into the boundary of God. The image that once opened the mystery begins to close it.

Perhaps this happens because an image that has revealed God to us becomes precious. If we first encountered divine love through the image of Father, we may fear that loosening our grip on that image means losing what we found there. If we encountered God through Scripture, sacrament, silence, reason, beauty, or community, we may begin to believe that everyone must enter through the same door.

But a window does not need to become the sun in order to carry its light. It only needs to remain transparent. Truth is sown everywhere. Many see it being sown, though few see it being harvested. Perhaps the seeds are scattered more widely than we imagine. Some fall into words we recognize immediately. Others fall into unfamiliar soil. Some appear in places where we have been trained not to look.

If there is only one Truth, perhaps we do not need to be afraid every time it appears wearing unfamiliar clothing. We can learn to recognize its fragrance.

Does this lead toward love? Does it make what was hidden more visible? Does it awaken life? Does it draw what has been divided toward wholeness? Does it make us more capable of seeing another person as something precious?

These questions do not eliminate discernment. Not every voice speaks truth simply because it claims to. Not every light leads home. But discernment is different from demanding that Truth always arrive in forms we already recognize.

Sometimes the unfamiliar image is precisely what allows us to see again. A word we have repeated for years suddenly opens. An old story reveals another layer. Something in nature becomes luminous. Another person's experience shows us a side of God our own life could never have taught us. The One has not changed. Our window has widened. Perhaps this is why we need many things.

No single image can contain the Father. No sentence can exhaust Christ. No doctrine can capture the fullness. Even our most treasured words remain invitations into something greater than themselves.

Truth keeps arriving. In bread and water. In light and love. In the familiar and the strange. In everything transparent enough to let the One shine through.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** One Truth,

> **Exhale:** teach me through all things.
