## Day 13 — The Gift of the Name

> “If someone goes down into the water and comes back up without receiving anything and says, ‘I'm a Christian,’ they borrowed the name with interest. But if they receive the Holy Spirit, they have the name as a gift. Those who receive a gift don't have to give it back, but those who borrow with interest have to pay.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

It is possible to carry a name without possessing its reality. A name can be inherited from our family, given to us by a community, or chosen because it describes the people among whom we belong. We can wear it for so long that eventually we stop asking what it means.

“I’m a Christian.”

For some, those words have been spoken since childhood. They may describe a church attended, beliefs affirmed, prayers learned, or a community that has shaped nearly every part of life. The name can become so familiar that it feels less like something received and more like something we have always owned.

But imagine borrowing the name. A borrowed name remains outside us. We can carry it, display it, defend it, and perhaps become very skilled at explaining what it means. Yet somewhere beneath all that activity remains the unsettling possibility that the word has described our affiliation more clearly than our transformation. The difference is not found simply in saying the name more confidently.

Consider the image of baptism. A person descends into water and rises again, an ancient picture of death and new life. The body has been washed, the ritual completed, and the community may now call the person Christian. Yet water can touch the skin without reaching the heart.

This is not an argument against ritual. Bread, water, oil, names, and images can all carry something sacred. But the visible act continually points beyond itself toward an invisible reality that must somehow be received. The name is meant to become a gift.

A gift is different from an achievement. We do not earn it by demonstrating that we deserve it, nor do we manufacture it through effort. We open our hands and allow another to place something there.

This returns us to faith. Faith receives, and what is received begins changing the one who receives it. The Spirit is not another badge placed beside the name Christian. The Spirit is breath, life, movement—the divine color soaking into the fibers until the name begins describing something that has actually become true.

Then *Christian* becomes less a label and more a direction of becoming. To bear the name of Christ is to allow the life we see in Christ to take form within us. Mercy begins to matter more than being right. Compassion becomes more natural than condemnation, and love becomes more important than maintaining the boundaries that assure us who belongs inside and who remains outside.

The name moves inward. Perhaps this is why names can be both dangerous and beautiful. A name is dangerous when we mistake possessing the word for possessing the reality. It becomes beautiful when it reminds us of the life into which we are continually being invited.

We may carry many such names. Believer. Disciple. Child of God. Beloved. Each can remain merely a word, or each can become a doorway.

Consider *beloved*. We can repeat that God loves us while living as though love must still be earned. We can sing about grace while privately believing that one more failure might finally exhaust it. The name has been spoken, but perhaps the gift has not yet been received.

Receiving takes time. Sometimes the distance between hearing that we are loved and actually knowing ourselves as loved can be measured in years. The mind understands long before the heart loosens its defenses.

The gift waits patiently. There is no shame in discovering that some of our names are still borrowed. Awareness itself is light entering the room. We do not need to tear off the name; we can allow the reality behind it to move deeper.

Perhaps the question is not, *Am I really a Christian?* Perhaps the gentler and more fruitful question is, *What would it mean for the name of Christ to become more real in me today?* Not someday when we have mastered ourselves, but here in the ordinary encounters through which love continually asks for a body. The answer may appear in something as small as listening more carefully, forgiving more freely, or refusing to turn another person's weakness into a verdict about their worth.

The gift becomes visible through living. Eventually the name and the life begin drawing closer together. We do not become perfect, and we do not cease needing mercy. We simply become less interested in proving that the name belongs to us and more interested in allowing its reality to shape us.

Perhaps then we can hold the name lightly. Not as a possession that separates us from others. Not as an achievement that proves we have arrived. But as a gift continually being received.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** I receive Your name.

> **Exhale:** Let it become life in me.
