## Day 27 — Becoming Like What We Love

> “Whatever a person loves, they resemble. If they love a woman, they resemble a woman. If they love a man, they resemble a man. If they love God, they resemble God.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Love changes the one who loves. Spend enough time with another person and something of them begins appearing in you. Their expressions find their way into your speech, their concerns become things you notice, and their joys begin mattering because they matter to someone you love. Intimacy leaves traces.

We become like what we love. This happens so gradually that we may not notice it. There is rarely a moment when we decide to take on the qualities of what holds our affection. Attention simply returns there again and again, and repetition slowly becomes formation.

Love teaches the eyes where to look. If we love recognition, we become attentive to status. We notice who is being praised, where we stand in comparison with others, and whether our contributions have been sufficiently acknowledged. The world slowly reorganizes itself around the question of how we are being seen.

If we love security above everything else, we become skilled at noticing threats. Uncertainty begins to feel intolerable, and anything we cannot control becomes a source of anxiety. We may accumulate money, information, plans, rules, or certainty in an attempt to build a world in which nothing unexpected can reach us. Yet the more we organize life around avoiding danger, the more danger becomes the thing we are always looking for.

Our loves train our attention. This does not mean every desire is a problem. We naturally love people, places, work, beauty, comfort, pleasure, achievement, and countless other good things. The question is not whether we love them but what kind of person our love is teaching us to become.

Every love carries an image of the good life. We move toward that image almost unconsciously. If success represents the good life, we organize ourselves around accomplishment. If belonging represents it, we may shape ourselves around whatever gains acceptance from the people whose approval matters most.

Eventually we begin resembling the altar at which we have been kneeling. Perhaps this is why transformation cannot be reduced to behavior. We can force ourselves to perform the right actions for a while, but beneath our actions remain the things we actually desire. Lasting change reaches deeper, into the loves from which behavior grows.

The heart must learn a different beauty. Fear can restrain us temporarily. Rules can tell us where the boundaries are, and discipline can help us practice choices that do not yet feel natural. But love changes what we want. That transformation is slower and deeper. 

Consider forgiveness. We can forgive because we believe we are required to, while inwardly continuing to rehearse the offense and wish for the other person to suffer. The behavior may be restrained, but the heart remains bound to the injury.

Then perhaps we encounter mercy. We discover ourselves forgiven in a place where we expected condemnation. Something softens, and mercy stops being merely an obligation we owe others. It becomes beautiful.

What we find beautiful becomes easier to desire. This may be why beholding matters so much. We saw earlier that to see Christ is to become Christ. Now the movement becomes even more intimate: we become like the One we love.

Attention becomes affection, and affection becomes likeness. If the God we love is primarily angry, suspicious, and eager to punish, we may gradually learn to resemble that image. We can become watchful of other people's failures, certain of our own righteousness, and comfortable with condemnation. We may call the resemblance holiness because we believe this is what God is like.

But look again at Christ. Look at the hands touching those others avoided. Watch the table expand to include people respectable religion regarded with suspicion, and notice the patience given to frightened disciples who repeatedly misunderstand. Listen to forgiveness being spoken even in the midst of violence.

If this is the face of the Father, then love has somewhere beautiful to rest. We do not need to force ourselves into Christlikeness as though carving a statue from unwilling stone. We can remain near what we love and allow resemblance to emerge. The life we contemplate begins quietly entering the life we live.

This is the work of years. We will still become angry, afraid, selfish, distracted, and tired. Old loves will continue competing for our attention, and some days the things we claim to value most will hardly seem visible in our choices. Transformation rarely travels in a straight line.

But love can be patient. A child often resembles a parent without trying to. The likeness is received through shared life, repeated presence, and the mysterious inheritance that comes from belonging. Perhaps becoming like the Father can be imagined with the same tenderness.

We remain near. We receive bread, sit in silence, practice mercy, return after failure, and keep turning our attention toward the Love we hope will shape us. Slowly, qualities that once felt foreign begin appearing within our ordinary reactions.

The resemblance emerges. We may notice that we are slower to condemn. Someone else's weakness awakens compassion before judgment, or an old resentment no longer carries the same heat. We become capable of generosity in a place where we once would have protected ourselves.

These are family resemblances. Perhaps holiness is less about appearing religious and more about becoming recognizable as children of Love. Not because we carry the right vocabulary or belong to the correct group, but because something of the Father's kindness has become visible in us.

What we love has left its likeness. So pay attention to your loves without condemning yourself for them. Notice where your imagination returns when it is free to wander, what losses frighten you most, and what promises seem to offer the life you most desire. These things can reveal the altars toward which the heart has been turning.

Then look again toward Love. Not with strain. Not with fear. Remain long enough to become what you behold.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Let me love what is true.

> **Exhale:** Make me like the Love I see.
