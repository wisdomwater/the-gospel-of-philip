## Day 21 — Water, Fire, and Light

> “It's through water and fire that the whole place is purified, the visible by the visible, the hidden by the hidden. There are some things hidden through the visible. There's water in water, there's fire in chrism.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Water touches the outside of the body. It runs across the skin, carries away dust, and leaves what it touches clean. Yet there is water hidden within water, something inward carried through an outward sign.

Fire moves differently. Water washes, but fire transforms. What passes through fire cannot always return to what it was before. Fire warms, illuminates, refines, and sometimes consumes, making it an image not simply of cleansing but of change.

Then there is oil. Chrism is oil placed upon the body, something tangible that can be felt on the skin. Yet fire is said to be hidden within it. The ordinary substance carries an invisible reality, just as bread can carry nourishment and the visible world can carry the hidden. Nothing is merely what it appears to be.

This is the language of sacrament. The physical thing remains physical, yet it becomes transparent to something deeper. Water remains water, oil remains oil, and bread remains bread, but each becomes capable of drawing our attention toward a reality that cannot be held in the hand.

Perhaps we have sometimes made sacraments too small. We can reduce them to ceremonies that must be performed correctly, administered by the right person, accompanied by the right words, and understood according to the right theology. These things may have their place, but the mystery is larger than the procedure. The visible act is meant to open into an invisible transformation.

Water must eventually become inner water. We can wash the body while leaving the heart untouched. We can pass through baptism while continuing to live from the same fears, resentments, judgments, and defenses that shaped us before. The outward washing becomes complete only as something within us begins to be washed as well.

Perhaps there are things within us that need water. Old shame may need to be washed from the names we call ourselves. Resentment may need to loosen its grip on memories we have replayed for years. The dust of old failures may need to be rinsed away so that yesterday no longer determines what we believe is possible today.

But some things need more than washing. There are patterns we do not simply want cleaned; we want them transformed. There are ways of seeing ourselves and others that have become so deeply woven into us that we cannot imagine life without them. Water touches them, but fire reaches deeper.

Fire asks what can remain. This does not need to be imagined as divine anger. The fire of God need not be the flame of punishment directed toward a frightened human being. It can be the fire of love that refuses to leave us imprisoned in what diminishes life.

Love can burn. It can burn through the illusion that we must earn our worth. It can consume the certainty that another person must remain forever defined by the worst thing they have done. It can expose the defenses we built for survival and show us that some of them are no longer needed. Yet this fire is hidden in oil.

There is tenderness in that image. Transformation does not always arrive violently, tearing through us in a dramatic moment of spiritual upheaval. Sometimes fire comes as something poured gently onto the skin.

Change can be quiet. A resentment loses some of its heat. A fear that once controlled every decision becomes something we can notice without obeying. We catch ourselves responding with compassion where judgment once appeared automatically.

The fire has been working beneath the surface. Then there is light. Fire gives light, but light does something more than change what it touches. It allows us to see. What was hidden in darkness becomes visible, and what seemed frightening when we could not see it clearly becomes something we can finally approach.

This is why these images belong together. Water cleanses what has accumulated. Fire transforms what has become fixed, and light reveals what has been hidden. Each describes a different movement of the same life working its way through the whole person.

The visible and hidden begin responding to one another. An outward act can awaken an inward reality, and an inward transformation can eventually become visible through outward life. Compassion hidden in the heart becomes a compassionate action. Forgiveness taking shape within becomes the decision not to return injury for injury.

The sacrament continues after the ceremony ends. Perhaps every ordinary day contains these movements. We are washed by experiences that allow us to begin again. We pass through fires that change us, and occasionally a light comes on inside us and we finally see something that had been present for years.

The sacred keeps appearing through the ordinary. A conversation can become water. Grief can become fire, and silence can become light. Even failure can become a sacrament when it reveals something true and opens us toward transformation.

This does not mean everything that happens is secretly good. Pain remains pain, injustice remains injustice, and loss does not need to be renamed as blessing before we are allowed to grieve it. But even within what we would never have chosen, something living can still meet us.

There can be water within water. There can be fire within oil. There can be light waiting inside darkness. Perhaps the invitation is simply to become attentive enough to notice. The whole world may be more sacramental than we imagined. Visible things are continually opening toward hidden realities, while hidden realities are continually seeking a body through which they can become visible.

Let the water wash you. Let the fire change you, and let the light show you what is true. Then allow what happens inwardly to find its way into the world through the life you actually live.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Wash me with Your life.

> **Exhale:** Kindle Your light within me.
