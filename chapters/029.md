# Day 29 — Digging Up the Root

> “As long as the root of evil is hidden, it's strong. But when it's recognized, it dissolves. When it's revealed, it perishes.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Some things grow best in darkness. A root can spread beneath the ground long before anything appears on the surface. We notice a reaction, a habit, or a recurring conflict and assume that what we can see is the problem. Yet the visible behavior may be only the branch of something growing much deeper.

We can spend years trimming branches. We promise not to react that way again, try harder to control an appetite, or tell ourselves we should stop feeling what we feel. Sometimes discipline helps, and there are behaviors that genuinely need to change. But when the same pattern keeps returning, perhaps something beneath it is still alive.

The root remains hidden. This can be frustrating because we often prefer problems that can be solved directly. If anger is the problem, stop being angry; if overeating is the problem, eat less; if anxiety is the problem, calm down. We identify the unwanted behavior and aim our effort directly at it.

But human beings are rarely that simple. Anger may have roots in fear. An appetite may be carrying loneliness, exhaustion, grief, or the longing for comfort. Anxiety may be growing around an old experience that taught the body to expect danger even when the mind knows it is safe.

The branch tells us where to dig. This changes our relationship with the things we struggle against. Instead of immediately declaring war on a behavior, we can become curious about what feeds it. We can ask what happens before it appears, what feeling accompanies it, and what promise it seems to make.

Awareness begins loosening the soil. At first, what we find may be uncomfortable. We may discover motives we would rather not admit, wounds we believed were long healed, or fears that do not fit the person we want to imagine ourselves to be. It can feel safer to leave the root underground.

Shame encourages us to do exactly that. Shame says, *Hide this before anyone sees what it says about you.* It pushes the unwanted thing deeper and covers the ground again. The root remains protected precisely because we are afraid to look at it.

Light works differently. Light says, *Let us see what is actually here.* It does not begin with a verdict, nor does it require us to decide whether what we find makes us good or bad. It simply reveals.

What is revealed can finally be understood. Perhaps the anger that embarrasses us once protected a younger self who had no other way to establish a boundary. Perhaps the need for approval developed when belonging felt uncertain, or the compulsion to achieve became a way of assuring ourselves that we had value. A pattern can be harmful now while still having begun as an attempt to survive.

Understanding does not excuse the harm. It gives us somewhere meaningful to work. When we know what the root has been seeking, we can begin offering something different. Fear can encounter safety, grief can be allowed to grieve, loneliness can seek connection, and shame can meet compassion. We stop demanding that the branch disappear while continuing to feed the root.

This is slow work. Roots intertwine with other roots. A behavior may carry years of memory, bodily habit, relationships, beliefs, and repeated reinforcement. Seeing the root once does not necessarily make it vanish.

But seeing changes the relationship. What was once operating entirely from darkness has entered awareness. We may still feel the impulse, but now we can recognize it arriving. A small space appears between what we feel and what we do.

Freedom often begins in that space. We do not need to become angry with the root. Hatred toward ourselves only creates another layer that will eventually need healing. We can dig carefully.

A gardener does not scream at a weed. The gardener learns what is growing, loosens the soil, and follows the root beneath the surface. Pulling only the visible leaves guarantees that the plant will return. Patient attention reaches deeper.

Perhaps repentance can look like this. Not merely regretting the branch, but becoming willing to uncover the root. We turn toward truth even when truth reveals something uncomfortable. We trust that what enters the light can become part of our healing.

This requires a particular image of God. If we believe God waits with disgust for our hidden life to be exposed, we will naturally keep burying it. Confession becomes dangerous because revelation seems likely to produce rejection. We learn to present the acceptable self even in prayer.

But what if the light is love? What if God already sees the root and has not turned away? What if the purpose of bringing something into the light is not to give God information, but to allow us to see ourselves within the mercy that has already seen us completely?

Then truth becomes safer. We can admit resentment without becoming resentment. We can acknowledge envy, fear, selfishness, desire, or shame without allowing any of them to become the final name for who we are. The pearl remains a pearl even while the mud is being washed away.

The root is not the whole person. This matters because inner work can itself become another obsession. We can spend our lives digging endlessly, convinced that somewhere beneath every thought is another defect requiring analysis. Self-knowledge was never meant to become self-surveillance.

The purpose of digging is freedom. We uncover what is hidden so that life can grow more freely. We make room for healthier roots: trust, compassion, courage, patience, honesty, and love. The field is not meant to remain forever under excavation. It is meant to bear fruit.

Perhaps this is why this image comes so near the end of our journey. We have encountered light, resurrection, anointing, self-knowledge, freedom, and love. Now all of them can accompany us into the places we once feared to see.

Nothing needs to remain hidden because nothing is beyond love. So when an old pattern appears, perhaps we can resist the urge to pronounce judgment. We can follow it downward with patience and ask what is feeding it. We can allow the light to reach the root.

What is hidden is strong. What is seen can change. And what is brought fully into love no longer needs the darkness in which it once survived.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Bring the hidden into light.

> **Exhale:** Heal me at the root.
