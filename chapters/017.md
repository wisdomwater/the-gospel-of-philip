# Day 17 — Making the Inside Like the Outside

> “He came to make some above and others below. Whoever was revealed here has that place above. Whoever has the hidden things is in the truth. The mysteries of truth are revealed as types and images.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

We divide the world easily. There is above and below, heaven and earth, sacred and ordinary, spirit and body. There is the person we appear to be and the person we know ourselves to be inside. Much of life is spent learning how to move between these worlds without allowing them to collide.

But perhaps salvation is not another division. Perhaps it is the healing of division. We know what it feels like when the inside and outside drift apart. We smile while angry, say yes while wanting to say no, appear confident while quietly afraid, and tell others we are fine while carrying grief we do not know how to name. None of this necessarily makes us dishonest; sometimes it is simply how human beings learn to survive. Yet the distance can become exhausting.

We begin maintaining a public self and protecting a private one. The outside learns what is expected while the inside carries questions, wounds, desires, and fears that seem too dangerous to reveal. Eventually we may no longer know which self is real.

Perhaps both are asking to become one. The same division can enter our spiritual lives. We develop a religious self who knows how to pray, what words to use, what doubts should remain unspoken, and what emotions belong in the presence of God. Meanwhile another self lives underneath, carrying anger, disappointment, desire, uncertainty, and sometimes even resentment toward the God we are supposed to love.

We may assume that spiritual maturity means silencing the second voice. But what if it means bringing the two together? Truth does not require us to become less human in order to approach God. If the hidden Father can be encountered within ordinary life, then perhaps even our unfinished places can become part of that encounter. Anger can be brought into prayer, questions can sit beside faith, and grief can remain grief without being hurried into an explanation.

The inside does not need to hide from the light. This is not the same as displaying every private thing to everyone. Wisdom still needs boundaries, and intimacy still requires trust. Integration does not mean exposure; it means no longer having to exile parts of ourselves from our own awareness. What is hidden can be held in truth.

Consider how much energy is required to fight ourselves. We condemn one desire, deny another emotion, hide a wound, perform confidence, and try to become the person we believe we ought to be. The inner world becomes crowded with guards protecting doors we are afraid to open.

Then light enters without accusation. What if nothing inside you needs to be hidden from God? The thought can be frightening because many of us learned to imagine God primarily as the One from whom our hidden life must be concealed. We know intellectually that God sees everything, yet emotionally we still attempt to present the acceptable version of ourselves. Prayer becomes another room in which we perform. But the hidden Father already inhabits the hidden places.

There is nowhere inside us that needs to be introduced to God. The fear, shame, anger, longing, tenderness, and confusion we carry are already known. The invitation is not to inform God of their existence but to allow ourselves to become present to what divine love has never stopped seeing.

This is how division begins to heal. The inside moves toward the outside, and the outside becomes more honest about the inside. The person we present to the world slowly begins resembling the person we actually are. We no longer need every room to be open to everyone, but we stop pretending certain rooms do not exist.

Above and below begin drawing nearer too. Heaven no longer needs to mean a distant spiritual realm disconnected from embodied life. The life of God can take shape here—in a conversation, a meal, an apology, an act of forgiveness, a moment of courage. What belongs “above” becomes visible “below.”

The invisible finds a body. This is what we have already seen in Christ. Compassion becomes hands touching someone considered unclean. Mercy becomes words spoken to someone expecting condemnation, and divine love becomes a human life moving among other human lives.

The hidden becomes visible without ceasing to be mystery. Perhaps this is what wholeness looks like. Spirit and body do not compete. Prayer and ordinary life stop occupying separate worlds, while the self we bring before God becomes the same self who goes to work, eats dinner, struggles, laughs, fails, loves, and begins again.

There is less distance to maintain. We may never achieve perfect integration. Human beings are layered creatures, and there will always be things within us we have not yet learned to see. Light continues revealing new rooms. But we can stop building new walls. Let the inside come into truth. Let the hidden meet the visible. Let what is above find expression below.

Perhaps salvation is not escape from the human life we have been given. Perhaps it is the slow gathering together of everything that has been divided until the whole person becomes transparent to love.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Gather what is divided.

> **Exhale:** Make me whole in You.
