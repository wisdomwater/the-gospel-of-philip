# Day 26 — Knowledge Makes Free; Love Makes Servant

> “Knowledge makes slaves free, but love never takes anything for itself, so it never enslaves anyone. Love doesn't say, ‘That's mine,’ but ‘That's yours.’”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Freedom can be imagined as having no one above us. We become free when no one controls our choices, dictates our direction, or possesses the power to tell us who we must be. Freedom appears as the removal of every chain.

There is truth in that. Knowledge can break chains because some forms of bondage depend upon our inability to see them. We cannot question a story we do not know we are telling ourselves, nor can we step outside a pattern we cannot recognize. When light enters, possibilities appear where inevitability once stood.

We have seen this within ourselves. A fear becomes visible, and suddenly it is no longer simply *reality*. We recognize the voice of shame and discover that its verdict is not the same thing as truth. We understand the wound beneath a reaction, and what once felt like an uncontrollable part of our personality becomes something we can approach with awareness.

Knowledge creates space. Within that space, freedom begins. We may still feel the old impulse, but we do not have to obey it automatically. We may hear the old accusation, but we no longer have to organize our lives around proving it wrong.

The chain has become visible. Yet freedom carries its own temptation. Once we have escaped something, we can begin looking down upon those who remain caught within it. The person who sees the chain may quietly become proud of being the one who sees.

Knowledge can free us from one master while handing us to another. We may become enslaved to being enlightened, correct, healed, awakened, or spiritually mature. We may need others to remain less aware so that we can continue experiencing ourselves as the ones who understand. Freedom becomes another hierarchy.

Love interrupts this. Love does not ask how freedom can elevate us above another person. It asks what our freedom makes possible for them. If our hands are no longer bound, perhaps they have been freed so that they can serve. This is a strange kind of freedom.

We usually imagine liberation as the ability to say, *I don't have to.* Love discovers the freedom to say, *I don't have to, but I choose to.* Service offered under compulsion is slavery; service offered freely can become love.

The distinction matters. A person who cannot say no does not truly possess a yes. When service grows from fear, guilt, obligation, or the need to be needed, it can slowly hollow us out. We give because we are afraid of what will happen if we stop.

Love gives differently. Love can say no when no is needed. It can establish boundaries, rest, withdraw from harm, and allow another person to carry responsibilities that belong to them. Love does not require self-erasure. But because love is free, it can also kneel.

There is no humiliation in bending down when no one is forcing us there. We can wash feet without becoming less ourselves. We can give time, attention, forgiveness, encouragement, food, money, or presence without turning the gift into evidence that the receiver now owes us something.

Love does not say, *That's mine.* This reaches deeper than possessions. We can treat people themselves as though they belong to us. We may speak of *my* spouse, *my* children, *my* friend, *my* congregation, *my* employee, and without noticing begin attaching ownership to relationship.

Love loosens its grip. The people we love remain other than us. They have paths we cannot walk for them, choices we cannot control, and inner worlds we can never completely enter. To love them is not to possess their future but to bless their freedom.

“That's yours.” Your life is yours. Your journey is yours, and your becoming is yours. I can walk beside you, care for you, speak truth, offer help, and sometimes carry you for a while, but love will not make your humanity into my possession.

Perhaps this is how the Father loves. Divine love does not seem frightened by human freedom. We are allowed to wander, misunderstand, resist, and learn slowly. Love keeps giving itself without turning the gift into coercion.

The sun shines without demanding repayment. Bread nourishes without asking the body to praise it. A spring gives water without becoming poorer because someone drinks. These images carry a generosity that does not calculate what will be returned.

This is the abundance from which love serves. If we believe there is never enough—enough worth, attention, recognition, security, or love—then giving can feel like losing. We clutch what we have because another person's gain seems to threaten our own. Scarcity teaches the heart to say, *Mine.*

But living food teaches another economy. What we receive from the Father becomes more alive as it moves through us. Mercy given away does not reduce the amount of mercy in the world. Compassion shared does not leave the giver with less compassion.

Love multiplies through release. Perhaps this is the destination of knowledge. We learn the truth so that we can become free, and we become free so that we can love without fear. Then love voluntarily returns us to the world we might have imagined freedom would allow us to escape. We become servants without becoming slaves. The difference is love.

A slave serves because another possesses them. A lover serves because they possess themselves enough to give themselves freely. The outward action may sometimes look similar, but inwardly they arise from entirely different worlds.

This may be one of the deepest signs of freedom. We no longer need every interaction to increase our status. We do not need to win every argument, receive credit for every contribution, or make another person's gratitude the price of our generosity. We can give and allow the gift to belong to the one who receives it.

Knowledge opens the hand. Love turns the open hand outward. And freedom discovers that its fullest expression may not be standing above anyone at all, but kneeling beside another person and saying, without fear of losing ourselves:

This is yours.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Set me free in Your love.

> **Exhale:** Make my freedom a gift.
