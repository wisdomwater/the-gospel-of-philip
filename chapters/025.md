# Day 25 — Know Yourself Completely

> “Those who know the truth are free. But those who are free don't sin, because ‘those who sin are slaves of sin.’ Truth is the mother, knowledge is the father. Those who think sinning doesn't apply to them are called ‘free’ by the world. Knowledge of the truth makes them arrogant, which means ‘it makes them free.’ It even makes them feel superior to the whole world. But ‘love builds up.’”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

There is a way of knowing that makes us larger in our own eyes. We discover something true, gain insight into ourselves or the world, and quietly begin separating humanity into those who understand and those who do not. Knowledge becomes another possession through which we establish our place above someone else.

But knowledge can also lead in the opposite direction.

The deeper we look into ourselves, the harder it becomes to maintain simple stories about who we are. We discover generosity beside selfishness, courage beside fear, compassion beside judgment. The human heart contains contradictions that resist being divided neatly into light and darkness.

To know ourselves is to encounter complexity.

We may begin with the person we believe ourselves to be. This identity has been assembled over years from family stories, successes, failures, wounds, expectations, and the reactions of other people. We learn which qualities earn approval and which ones should remain hidden.

Eventually the story becomes familiar enough to feel like the self.

Yet there is always more beneath it.

A person who thinks of himself as angry may eventually discover fear underneath the anger. Someone who believes she is lazy may find exhaustion, discouragement, or the conviction that failure is inevitable. What looked like a character defect begins revealing a history.

Understanding changes the relationship.

This does not mean every behavior becomes acceptable once we understand where it came from. We remain responsible for what we do and for the ways our actions affect other people. But responsibility becomes more useful when it grows from awareness rather than condemnation.

We can change what we are willing to see.

Shame works differently. Shame reduces the whole person to the behavior and then declares the case closed. *I failed, therefore I am a failure; I acted selfishly, therefore selfishness is what I am.*

Knowledge opens the case again.

It asks what happened before the behavior, what we were feeling, what we feared, and what we were trying to obtain or avoid. It notices patterns without turning patterns into identity. The question changes from *What is wrong with me?* to *What is happening within me?*

That small change can create room for freedom.

A pattern that remains invisible feels inevitable. We find ourselves repeating the same reaction and wondering why it keeps happening. Once the pattern becomes visible, however, a space begins appearing between the impulse and the response.

Light enters the machinery.

Perhaps this is why self-knowledge belongs beside truth. Truth is not only knowledge about God, the universe, scripture, or spiritual mysteries. Truth also includes the willingness to see ourselves without exaggeration in either direction.

We are neither as terrible as shame tells us nor as complete as pride would like us to believe.

We are unfinished.

There is freedom in admitting this. We no longer need to maintain an image of ourselves as unusually good, unusually spiritual, unusually enlightened, or unusually broken. We can become ordinary human beings in the process of becoming whole.

Then knowledge becomes gentler.

The more honestly we see ourselves, the more difficult it becomes to condemn another person from a distance. We recognize something of our own fear in their defensiveness, our own hunger in their grasping, and our own wounds in the ways they protect themselves. Understanding ourselves can become a doorway into compassion.

This is where love builds up.

Knowledge alone can become another hierarchy. We know the right theology, understand the deeper mystery, have done the inner work, or possess an insight others have not yet discovered. The ego is remarkably capable of turning even awakening into a reason for superiority.

Love asks a different question.

Not *What do I know that they do not?* but *What does what I know make possible for love?* If insight makes us colder, more dismissive, or more certain of our superiority, something has gone wrong. Truth should eventually increase our capacity for communion.

Self-knowledge can do the same.

When we discover our own wounds, we become more careful with the wounds of others. When we recognize how slowly some patterns change, we may become more patient with another person's unfinished places. Mercy becomes easier when we remember how much of it we ourselves require.

The pearl remains within all of this.

Knowing ourselves completely does not mean cataloging everything that is wrong with us. It also means discovering the treasure we have overlooked. There may be courage beneath the fear, tenderness beneath the defenses, and a capacity for love that years of self-protection have kept hidden.

We can become strangers to our goodness as easily as to our wounds.

Some people find it easier to confess failure than to receive a compliment. They can describe every weakness in detail while struggling to name what is beautiful within them. Humility becomes confused with refusing to acknowledge the gifts that are actually there.

But truth does not require distortion.

If there is kindness in you, it can be seen. If there is courage, creativity, tenderness, patience, or wisdom, these do not need to be denied in order to remain humble. A gift can be recognized without becoming a possession through which we establish superiority.

We receive it with gratitude.

Perhaps knowing ourselves completely means holding all of this together. We see the wounds without becoming the wounds, the gifts without becoming proud, the desires without becoming enslaved to them, and the failures without turning them into final verdicts.

We become more transparent to ourselves.

And perhaps, beneath everything we discover, there is something still deeper. Beneath the roles, defenses, appetites, achievements, failures, and stories is the person already known by the Father. Before we finish understanding ourselves, we are understood.

Self-knowledge eventually becomes surrender.

We stop trying to manufacture an acceptable self and become willing to meet the person who is actually here. We bring that person into the light and discover that the light does not recoil.

Truth reveals.

Knowledge makes visible.

And love builds up what it sees.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Let me see myself truly.

> **Exhale:** Teach me to love what You see.
