# Day 10 — Faith Receives, Love Gives

> “Faith receives, love gives. Nobody can receive without faith. Nobody can give without love. So we believe in order to receive, but in order to truly give, we love.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

There is a rhythm to the spiritual life.

We receive, and we give. We breathe in, and we breathe out. Something comes to us as gift, settles into our life, and eventually begins looking for somewhere else to go.

Faith receives. Love gives.

We sometimes make faith much more complicated than this. Faith becomes agreement with a collection of ideas, certainty about things we cannot see, or the ability to silence questions that make us uncomfortable. We measure faith by how confidently someone can say, *I know.*

But perhaps faith begins more simply.

Faith opens its hands.

To receive anything, we must first admit that we do not already possess everything we need. The hungry person receives bread because hunger has created an empty place. The thirsty person receives water because thirst has taught the body that it cannot sustain itself alone. Receiving begins with openness.

This can be difficult for those of us who have learned to value self-sufficiency. We would often rather earn than receive, because earning leaves us in control. If something has been earned, we can point to the work that justified it, but a gift leaves us with nothing to point toward except the generosity of the giver.

Grace can therefore feel strangely uncomfortable.

We want to know what we must do to deserve love, forgiveness, belonging, or peace. Give us the requirements and we will get to work. We can improve ourselves, discipline ourselves, measure ourselves, and compare today's performance with yesterday's, because effort feels safer than simply opening our hands.

But the pearl was valuable before it was cleaned.

Perhaps faith begins there. It receives what cannot be earned: the possibility that we are already held, already seen, already invited into life. Faith does not manufacture the gift; it simply stops closing its hands against it.

Yet receiving is only half the rhythm.

Love gives.

If faith continually receives but never gives, the gift becomes something we try to possess. Grace becomes *my* grace, truth becomes *my* truth, salvation becomes *my* salvation, and even God can become another treasure accumulated for ourselves. What began as abundance slowly becomes ownership.

Love breaks open the container.

What has been received begins moving outward. The mercy we needed becomes mercy offered to someone else. The patience once extended toward our confusion becomes patience with another person's unfinished story. The forgiveness that released us from our worst moment makes us slower to imprison someone else inside theirs.

Love gives because love has discovered abundance.

This is different from giving out of obligation. Many of us know what it is to give because we are afraid of disappointing someone, because we want to appear generous, or because we hope our generosity will eventually be returned. We can empty ourselves while quietly keeping an account of what everyone owes us.

Love does not keep the same ledger.

It gives because something living has begun overflowing. A spring does not calculate how much water it can release before the landscape deserves more. Light does not decide which part of the room has earned illumination. The nature of abundance is movement.

Perhaps this is why faith must come first.

If we have never learned to receive, giving can become exhausting. We try to love from emptiness, serve from resentment, forgive through clenched teeth, and offer ourselves long after we have lost contact with the source from which love comes. Eventually even generosity can become another way of proving our worth.

Faith returns us to the source.

Receive.

Let yourself be loved before trying to become more lovable. Receive mercy before attempting to manufacture mercy for everyone else. Allow yourself to be nourished by the bread from heaven before worrying about how much bread you can distribute.

Then give.

The rhythm needs both movements. Breathing in without breathing out cannot sustain life, and breathing out without breathing in cannot sustain it either. Receiving and giving belong to the same living movement.

Perhaps even our relationship with God participates in this rhythm. We receive life from the Father, and then the life we receive becomes love moving through us toward the world. What comes from God returns to God by passing through another human being.

A meal prepared. A wound forgiven. A lonely person noticed. A frightened person reassured. A stranger treated as though their life carries the same hidden treasure as ours.

None of these things needs to be dramatic. Love often gives itself in remarkably ordinary forms, just as heaven once appeared to us as bread. The smallest act can become part of the great circulation of gift.

Faith receives.

Love gives.

And somewhere between the open hands that receive and the open hands that offer, we begin discovering what abundance actually means. We were never meant merely to possess the life we have been given.

We were meant to let it flow.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** I receive Your love.

> **Exhale:** Let Your love flow through me.
