# Day 11 — The Divine Dyer

> “God is a dyer. Just as good dyes, which are called ‘true,’ dissolve into what is dyed in them, so it is with those God dyes. Since his dyes are immortal, they become immortal through his colors.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Imagine cloth lowered slowly into a vat of dye. At first the color touches only the surface, but soon it begins working its way through every fiber. Leave the cloth there long enough and the distinction between the fabric and the color becomes difficult to make.

God is a dyer. It is an unexpected image for transformation because we often imagine spiritual growth as something we accomplish through effort. We identify what is wrong, make a plan, practice better behavior, and measure whether we are improving. There is value in discipline, but dye works differently.

The cloth does not strain to become blue. It is immersed. Perhaps transformation begins with allowing ourselves to remain in the presence of what we hope to become. Yesterday we encountered faith as receiving and love as giving, a rhythm that begins with open hands rather than clenched effort. Today the image becomes even more passive and intimate: before we do anything with the color, we allow ourselves to be submerged in it.

This may be one of the quiet purposes of prayer. We often come to prayer carrying words. We have requests to make, problems to solve, people to remember, failures to confess, and questions we hope will finally receive answers. There is nothing wrong with any of this, but sometimes prayer can be simpler than speaking.

Sometimes we sit in the dye. We place ourselves in the presence of Love without trying to accomplish anything. We breathe, become quiet, and allow ourselves to be held without evaluating whether the moment is productive. Nothing dramatic may happen, yet something can still be soaking into us.

The deepest colors often appear slowly. Perhaps this is why transformation can be difficult to notice while it is happening. We expect sudden breakthroughs, but much of spiritual growth may look like ordinary days accumulating upon one another. We pray, forgive, fail, begin again, notice beauty, practice compassion, and slowly discover that our reactions are not quite what they once were. Something has changed color.

A situation that once provoked immediate anger now contains a small space before the reaction. Someone we once dismissed becomes easier to see with compassion. A failure that would once have produced days of shame becomes something we can examine, learn from, and release.

We may not know exactly when the change occurred. The color has entered the fibers. This image also invites us to consider what else we are soaking in. Human beings are remarkably absorbent creatures. The conversations we repeat, the voices we trust, the images we consume, and the fears we rehearse all leave traces of color behind.

Spend enough time immersed in outrage and the world begins to look outrageous. Spend enough time immersed in comparison and almost every encounter becomes a measurement of where we stand. Spend enough time rehearsing our inadequacy and even kindness can begin to feel suspicious. We may believe we are simply observing the world when we are actually being dyed by it.

This is why attention matters. We have already encountered the startling claim that we become what we truly see. The dyer gives us another image of the same mystery. What surrounds us long enough eventually begins entering us.

So what color is God? Perhaps there is no single answer. Mercy has a color, and so does courage. Compassion, patience, joy, truth, tenderness, and peace each reveal something of the divine life, like different shades emerging from the same inexhaustible source. Christ wears these colors in human flesh.

Look long enough at the way he treats the unwanted and compassion begins to acquire a shape. Watch him forgive and mercy becomes visible. See him refuse to answer violence with violence, and another color appears—one strong enough to endure hatred without becoming hateful.

These are not colors meant merely to be admired. They are meant to soak into us. Perhaps this is why the dye is called immortal. What enters deeply enough becomes part of something death cannot finally erase. The love we receive and give, the mercy that changes a relationship, the compassion that allows another person to feel seen—these participate in a life larger than the temporary forms through which they appear.

We are being colored by eternity. There is no need to rush the process. Cloth pulled from the dye too quickly may carry only a faint stain, while cloth allowed to remain becomes saturated. Perhaps some things can only happen through staying.

Stay with love. Stay with mercy, even when judgment would be easier. Stay with silence when nothing seems to be happening. Stay before the face of Christ until the qualities you see there begin appearing quietly within your own.

God is a dyer, and you are already in the water. The invitation is not to strain harder but to remain. Let the color reach the places that effort alone has never been able to change. Let love move beyond the surface until it becomes difficult to tell where the gift ends and you begin.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Immerse me in Your love.

> **Exhale:** Color me from within.
