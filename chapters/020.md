# Day 20 — Putting On the Light

> “Those who put on the perfect light can't be seen by the powers, nor can they be detained. But they'll put it on sacramentally in the union.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

There is a difference between standing in the light and wearing it. We can stand beneath the sun and remain ourselves, illuminated for a moment by something outside us. But to put on light suggests something more intimate: what surrounds us has somehow become part of us.

The image carries us back into the bridal chamber. Union is not merely closeness between two who remain untouched by one another. Something is exchanged there, and the one who enters does not leave exactly as they came. They come out wearing light.

We usually think of clothing as something that conceals us. We dress the body and decide what others will be allowed to see, sometimes choosing clothing that communicates the person we want to appear to be. Clothing can become another boundary between the hidden self and the visible one.

But light conceals nothing. To put on light is to become visible without being exposed. Nothing needs to hide because nothing is being accused. The light reveals what is there while somehow freeing us from the fear of being seen.

Perhaps that is why the powers cannot detain the one who wears it. Whatever these powers may have meant within Philip’s spiritual world, we know something about the forces that hold human beings captive. Fear can hold us, shame can hold us, resentment can hold us, and the endless need for approval can hold us.

Some prisons do not require walls. A person can move freely through the world while remaining imprisoned by what others might think. We can organize our choices around avoiding rejection, failure, embarrassment, or loss. The power does not need to command us aloud because we have already learned to anticipate its judgment.

Light interrupts this arrangement. What power does shame have over someone who has already allowed themselves to be completely seen and completely loved? What leverage does approval have over someone whose worth no longer rises and falls with applause? The old powers may continue speaking, but their voices begin losing authority.

The light does not destroy them by fighting them. It simply reveals that they are not ultimate. This is a different kind of freedom than becoming invulnerable. We can still be hurt, misunderstood, rejected, or disappointed. Putting on light does not turn a human being into someone beyond pain.

It gives pain a larger place in which to exist. The pearl can fall into mud without becoming mud. The body can carry hunger without becoming hunger, and the heart can experience fear without becoming fear. Now the person clothed in light can pass through darkness without concluding that darkness is the deepest truth about who they are.

The light has gone deeper than circumstance. Perhaps this is why the light must be *put on*. It is not enough to agree that divine love exists somewhere in the universe. We must allow ourselves to become clothed in it until love becomes the place from which we meet everything else.

Imagine beginning the day this way. Before putting on the roles that wait for us—worker, parent, spouse, friend, caregiver, leader, helper—we put on light. Before remembering our responsibilities, failures, unfinished tasks, or the expectations other people carry for us, we remember the deeper identity beneath all of them. We are held in a love we did not manufacture.

Then we walk into the day from there. The difficult conversation may still be difficult. The mistake may still need to be corrected, and the wound may still need attention. But these things encounter someone who is no longer entirely dependent upon them for a verdict about their worth.

Light becomes a kind of inner freedom. This does not make us indifferent to how we affect others. In fact, the opposite may happen. When less energy is required for defending ourselves, more becomes available for seeing the person in front of us.

We can listen without immediately preparing a defense. We can apologize without believing an apology makes us worthless. We can be corrected without collapsing into shame, and we can disagree without needing to turn another person into an enemy. The light gives us enough room to remain present.

Perhaps this is what union produces. We entered the bridal chamber seeking closeness with God, but we emerge carrying something of the encounter into the world. The hidden communion becomes visible in the way we inhabit our own lives.

We wear what we have received. This is not another spiritual performance. Light worn as a costume quickly becomes another form of darkness, forcing us to appear peaceful, loving, certain, or holy when we are none of those things. Perfect light does not require pretending. It makes pretending unnecessary.

There will still be shadows within us. There will be fears we have not understood, wounds that remain tender, desires that pull in conflicting directions, and places where love has not yet reached its full expression. Light allows these things to be seen without allowing them to become our identity.

Nothing needs to be hidden from love. Perhaps the light is perfect not because we are perfect, but because the Source of the light is complete. We do not generate it through moral effort or spiritual achievement. Like everything else we have encountered, it is received. And what is received begins becoming what we are.

So put on the light. Let it rest over the places where shame once spoke most loudly. Let it enter the rooms where fear has been making decisions, and let it illuminate the names you have mistaken for yourself.

Then walk into the world wearing what you have received. The powers may still speak. But they no longer get to name you.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Clothe me in Your light.

> **Exhale:** Let me walk in freedom.
