# Day 15 — Truth Comes Clothed in Images

> “Truth didn't come into the world naked, but it came in types and images. The world won't receive it in any other way.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

Truth does not come naked. It arrives wearing something we recognize. It becomes bread placed into our hands, water touching our skin, light entering a darkened room, seed disappearing beneath the soil. The invisible clothes itself in the visible so that we have somewhere to begin.

Perhaps we need truth to come this way because some realities are too large to receive directly. Try explaining love without telling a story, describing a gesture, or remembering a face. We reach instinctively for images because an image can carry something that a definition cannot.

The world receives truth through things. This does not make the things unimportant. Bread matters precisely because it can be eaten, water because it can wash, oil because it can soak into the skin, and light because it can be seen. The physical world becomes capable of carrying meaning without ceasing to be physical. A doorway is still wood and hinges, but it is also an invitation to enter.

We have already encountered many such doorways. The pearl showed us a value that mud cannot diminish. The dyer showed us transformation through immersion, while bread taught us that what is received can become part of who we are.

None of these images contains the whole truth. That is part of their beauty. An image reveals by remaining incomplete, giving us something real without pretending to give us everything. Bread can teach us nourishment, but it cannot tell us everything about love; light can teach us illumination, but it cannot tell us everything about belonging.

So another image comes. And then another. Father and Son. Bride and bridegroom. Water and fire. Death and resurrection. Each opens a window onto the same mystery from another direction.

The danger begins when we mistake the clothing for the one wearing it. We can become attached to an image because it once revealed something beautiful to us. The image becomes precious, and eventually we may begin protecting it as though questioning the image would threaten the truth itself. What was meant to open a doorway can quietly become a wall.

Religious language is especially vulnerable to this. We say *Father* and imagine we have described God. We say *kingdom*, *salvation*, *heaven*, *cross*, or *resurrection* and generations of interpretation immediately gather around the words. The images may be true, yet Truth remains greater than the meanings we have learned to attach to them.

Truth wears the garment without becoming the garment. Perhaps this is why mystery is not the enemy of faith. Mystery is what remains when we have followed an image as far as it can take us and discovered that reality continues beyond it. The failure is not in the image; the image has done exactly what it was meant to do. It brought us to the edge.

There we may be tempted to construct another explanation immediately. We are uncomfortable with not knowing, so we reach for another word, another doctrine, another system that will make the mystery manageable. Yet sometimes the most faithful response to an image is simply to allow it to become transparent. Look through it.

Bread becomes nourishment, and nourishment becomes Christ. Water becomes cleansing, and cleansing becomes new birth. Light becomes seeing, and seeing becomes transformation.

Eventually the image disappears into what it reveals. This can change the way we encounter the ordinary world. If truth comes clothed in images, then perhaps the sacred is not confined to explicitly religious things. The world itself can become a collection of garments through which something deeper occasionally becomes visible.

Rain falling against a window can speak. So can the smell of bread, the warmth of sunlight, the silence before dawn, or the hand of someone who remains beside us when there is nothing useful left to say. None of these things needs to become a secret code in which God is hiding messages for us. They can simply become places where attention opens into wonder.

The visible world becomes transparent. Perhaps this is also how we can approach the stranger images ahead. Bridal chamber, chrism, union, male and female, water and fire—some may feel distant from our ordinary religious language. We do not need to force them immediately into familiar categories.

We can let them speak before deciding what they mean. Hold the image gently. Turn it over. Notice what it awakens and where it resists easy explanation. Allow it to lead toward mystery rather than demanding that it surrender a definition.

Truth did not come naked. Perhaps that is mercy. The Infinite clothes itself in things small enough for human hands, human eyes, and human imagination. It comes near without ceasing to be mystery, offering enough light for the next step without illuminating the entire horizon.

Receive the garment. But keep looking through it. Something more is shining underneath.

## Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Open the image.

> **Exhale:** Show me what is hidden.
