## Day 16 — The Father Within

> “It's through water and fire that the whole place is purified, the visible by the visible, the hidden by the hidden. There are some things hidden through the visible. There's water in water, there's fire in chrism.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

There are things hidden inside things. Water appears to be only water, yet another water may be hidden within it. Oil rests upon the skin, yet fire may be concealed inside the oil. The visible world carries something invisible without ceasing to be what it is.

Perhaps we have been searching for God in the wrong direction. We instinctively look upward and outward. God is somewhere beyond the sky, beyond the world, beyond ourselves, inhabiting a spiritual realm separated from ordinary existence. Prayer then becomes an attempt to cross the distance between here and there.

But what if the distance is not what we imagined? There is a hiddenness that does not mean absence. A seed hidden beneath the soil is not far away; it is beneath our feet. Breath hidden within the body is closer still, moving through us before we even remember to notice it. The hidden can be nearer than the visible.

This changes the way we might imagine the Father. We do not need to picture a distant ruler watching creation from somewhere outside it. The Source from whom all things come may also be the Presence within whom all things continually exist. The Father is hidden, but hidden does not mean gone.

Consider your own awareness for a moment. Thoughts appear and disappear within it, emotions rise and fall, sensations move through the body, and memories unexpectedly surface. Beneath all this movement is the simple fact that you are here, aware that you are alive.

Stay there for a moment. Before the explanations, before the labels, before the story you tell about yourself, there is presence. You do not have to manufacture it. You simply notice what was already here.

Perhaps prayer can begin with that noticing. We may come to prayer believing we need to summon God into the room. We search for the right words, the right feeling, or some unmistakable sign that our prayer has reached its destination. When nothing dramatic happens, we wonder whether anyone is listening.

But perhaps God was never waiting to be summoned. The One we seek may already be present beneath the seeking. The longing itself may be carrying something of the One for whom we long. Even our awareness of absence may exist within a Presence deeper than our ability to perceive it.

There is water in water. There is fire in oil. There may be God hidden within the ordinary experience of being alive.

This does not mean that we are God in some simple sense, nor that every thought or desire arising within us is divine. There is much within us that remains confused, wounded, frightened, and unfinished. Yet the pearl can lie in mud without becoming mud. Something precious remains.

Perhaps the work of contemplation is less about reaching a distant God and more about becoming quiet enough to notice the Presence already here. We descend beneath the noise of our thoughts, beneath the identities we defend, beneath the endless commentary about whether we are succeeding or failing. We do not destroy these things; we simply stop mistaking them for the deepest part of ourselves.

Then silence begins to feel different. At first silence may seem empty because the usual signals are missing. There is no voice, no vision, no emotional surge announcing that something spiritual is happening. Yet if we remain, emptiness can slowly become spaciousness. The hidden begins to feel inhabited.

This can change how we return to the world. If the Father is encountered only in special places, then ordinary life continually pulls us away from God. But if the hidden Presence can be found within ordinary things, then washing dishes, walking beneath trees, listening to another person, or sitting quietly at the end of a long day can all become places of encounter.

The sacred does not need to interrupt the ordinary. It can shine through it. Yesterday we learned that Truth comes clothed in images. Today the clothing becomes thinner. The visible does not merely point toward something far away; it may carry within itself a hidden depth.

Perhaps this is what the images have been trying to teach us all along. Bread carries nourishment. Water carries cleansing. Oil carries fire. A human being carries an interior universe, and Christ carries the hidden name of the Father.

Everything opens inward. So perhaps we do not need to climb toward heaven tonight. We can become still where we are. We can notice the breath entering and leaving the body and allow ourselves to rest beneath the endless need to understand.

The Father is hidden. And the hidden may be very near.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** You are already here.

> **Exhale:** Let me rest within You.
