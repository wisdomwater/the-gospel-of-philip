## Day 7 — The Treasure in the Body

> “There's a human inside a human, and the whole universe is inside them.”
>
> — *The Gospel of Philip*

There is more within you than can be seen from the outside. A body enters a room, occupies a chair, walks down a street, grows tired, becomes hungry, and eventually grows old. From the outside, a human life can seem remarkably ordinary, yet within that ordinary body is a world no one else can completely enter.

There is a human inside the human. Within you are memories no photograph can fully recover, fears you may never have spoken aloud, and loves whose depth cannot be measured from the outside. There are childhood rooms you can still walk through in memory and voices of people long gone that you can almost hear when the world becomes quiet. An entire interior landscape travels with you everywhere you go.

And somehow, the whole universe is there too. The stars contain elements that eventually became bone and blood, while the earth beneath our feet becomes food and then becomes flesh. Air enters the lungs, water moves through the body, and sunlight captured by plants becomes energy within our cells. The boundary between ourselves and the world has never been quite as solid as it appears. Yet the treasure hidden within us is more than matter.

There is awareness itself, this mysterious capacity to know that we are here. We can stand beneath a night sky and not merely see stars but experience wonder. We can hear another person describe their pain and somehow feel compassion arise within our own body, crossing the invisible distance between one interior world and another.

What an extraordinary thing to carry inside something so vulnerable. Bodies bruise. They become sick and tired, change with age, and refuse our attempts to control them completely. We may judge them by their appearance, compare them with other bodies, resent their limitations, or treat them as projects that will finally become acceptable once the right number appears on a scale, a mirror, or a medical report.

But a body is more than an object to evaluate. It is the place where the hidden becomes visible. Love needs hands if it is going to prepare a meal for someone who is hungry. Compassion needs feet if it is going to cross the room and sit beside someone who is alone. Music needs lungs and fingers, laughter needs breath, and an embrace needs arms. The invisible life within us continually asks the body to give it form.

Perhaps this is one reason spiritual life cannot simply mean escaping the physical. Whatever treasure has been entrusted to us is carried here, in flesh. Prayer happens through breathing lungs, kindness through moving bodies, communion through bread and wine, baptism through water touching skin.

The sacred keeps appearing in matter. This can be difficult to remember because we often divide ourselves into pieces. We imagine a spiritual part that God cares about and a physical part that must be managed, restrained, improved, or tolerated. Yet our deepest experiences rarely respect this division: grief tightens the chest, anxiety knots the stomach, joy brings tears, and peace changes the rhythm of our breathing.

The human inside and the human outside belong together. Perhaps healing begins when we stop treating the body as an obstacle to spiritual life and begin receiving it as a place of encounter. We can listen when it is tired, notice when it is frightened, feed it when it is hungry, and allow it to rest without turning every need into a moral judgment. Attention itself can become a form of compassion.

There is humility in this because the treasure is carried in something fragile. We cannot make the body permanent, and eventually each of us must surrender the form through which we have known this world. Yet fragility does not diminish value; sometimes it is precisely what teaches us to recognize it.

A clay cup can still carry living water. Perhaps this is why we should look more gently at one another as well. Every person we pass is carrying an interior universe we cannot see. The impatient cashier, the distracted coworker, the stranger driving too slowly in front of us, the person whose opinions make no sense to us—all contain histories, wounds, desires, memories, and possibilities hidden beneath the surface.

There is a human inside the human. To remember this is already an act of love.

We have spent these first days asking what it means to inherit the living, to move beyond names, to recognize Truth appearing through many things, to receive bread from heaven, and to discover resurrection before death. Perhaps all of these movements have been leading us inward as much as upward. The life we seek is not merely somewhere beyond us.

The treasure has been placed surprisingly close. It breathes when we breathe, aches when we ache, and opens its eyes each morning upon a world filled with things that can still astonish us. Beneath all the labels we have accumulated and all the judgments we have made about ourselves, something remains quietly capable of receiving light.

There is a human inside the human. And there is more here than we know.

### Breath Prayer

> **Inhale:** Your treasure lives within.

> **Exhale:** Teach me to see.
