# The Gospel of Philip: A Modern Devotional

*A 30-day contemplative journey through one of early Christianity’s most mysterious texts.*

## About This Project

The *Gospel of Philip* is an early Christian collection of sayings, reflections, and symbolic teachings preserved among the writings discovered near Nag Hammadi, Egypt, in 1945.

Unlike the narrative gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, Philip does not tell a continuous story of the life of Jesus. Instead, it moves through images and fragments: bread and water, oil and fire, pearls and light, names and mirrors, resurrection, anointing, and the mysterious bridal chamber.

This project is a modern contemplative reading of those images.

Across thirty daily readings, passages from the *Gospel of Philip* become starting points for reflection on transformation, identity, desire, healing, resurrection, divine love, self-knowledge, and union with God. Each reading concludes with a simple breath prayer intended to move the reader from reflection into stillness.

## What This Book Is

This book is a devotional encounter with the *Gospel of Philip*.

It is written for readers who may be encountering Philip for the first time, as well as those already interested in contemplative Christianity, Christian mysticism, early Christian writings, or the Nag Hammadi library. No previous knowledge of Valentinian Christianity or early Christian history is required.

The goal is not to remove Philip's strangeness. Its ancient images are allowed to remain mysterious while being placed in conversation with the ordinary experiences of contemporary life.

At the heart of these readings is a recurring idea: spiritual truth is not merely something to understand. It is something in which we participate.

We receive the bread.

We enter the water.

We are anointed with oil.

We put on the light.

And, slowly, we become like what we behold.

## What This Book Is Not

This is not a new scholarly translation of the *Gospel of Philip*, nor is it intended as an academic commentary or reconstruction of Valentinian theology.

The meditations are interpretive and contemplative. They explore what Philip's imagery might open within a modern reader while recognizing that the text emerged from a very different historical and theological world.

Readers interested in the historical *Gospel of Philip* are encouraged to read the complete ancient text alongside these meditations and to explore scholarly work on Nag Hammadi Christianity and Valentinian thought.

## A Note on the Source Text

The surviving *Gospel of Philip* is preserved in Coptic as part of Codex II of the Nag Hammadi collection. The work is generally associated with the Valentinian stream of early Christianity and contains distinctive reflections on sacraments, resurrection, knowledge, union, and the relationship between visible and hidden realities.

This project uses a public-domain English translation of the ancient text as the basis for the passages quoted throughout the devotional.

Because this is a contemplative adaptation rather than a translation, the reflections that follow each passage should not be treated as statements about the only possible historical meaning of the text. They represent one modern encounter with Philip's imagery.

## How to Read

Philip rewards slow reading.

Some passages may immediately resonate. Others may feel strange, confusing, or resistant to interpretation. There is no need to resolve every image before moving forward.

Allow the images to remain with you.

Notice which ones return during the day. Sit with the breath prayer for a few moments after reading. Pay attention to what becomes illuminated rather than worrying about whether you have completely understood the text.

The purpose is not mastery.

It is encounter.

## Central Themes

Several themes recur throughout the thirty readings: resurrection as a present reality, the transformation of desire, the dignity of the body, self-knowledge without shame, freedom expressed through love, the hidden divine presence within ordinary life, and the movement from separation toward communion.

Perhaps most important is Philip's language of participation. Truth is not simply believed. Light is put on, bread is eaten, oil is received, resurrection is entered, and the one who beholds Christ begins to become like what is seen.

The visible becomes a doorway into the hidden.

## About This Adaptation

This work was created as part of an ongoing project to bring ancient spiritual texts into conversation with contemporary contemplative life.

The intention is neither to reproduce ancient Christianity unchanged nor to reshape it until it comfortably agrees with modern assumptions. Instead, the project attempts to listen carefully across the distance between Philip's world and ours, allowing both its beauty and its strangeness to remain.

Where these readings emphasize mercy, healing, participation, and divine love, they do so through a contemplative theological lens. Readers are invited to engage the original text, question these interpretations, and discover what Philip's images awaken in them.
