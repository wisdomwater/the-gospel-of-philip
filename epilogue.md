# Epilogue

## Go in the Light

We have come to the end of these readings, but perhaps that is not quite the right way to describe it. Philip has spent thirty days teaching us to distrust simple endings. Seeds disappear before they rise, what is hidden becomes visible, and resurrection begins before death.

So there is nothing more you need to understand before closing this book.

You do not need to solve the bridal chamber or explain the hidden name. You do not need to decide exactly what Philip meant by every image or fit his strange and beautiful fragments into a perfectly ordered theology. Some things are allowed to remain mysterious.

Carry the images instead.

When you feel diminished by failure, remember the pearl in the mud. When an old pattern appears again, follow the branch gently toward its hidden root. When you find yourself trying to earn what can only be received, remember that faith opens its hands.

When the world feels ordinary, look again.

There may be water within water and fire within oil. Bread may become more than bread, and another human being may contain an interior universe you cannot see. The sacred may be nearer than you imagined.

When darkness comes, do not assume the light has disappeared.

There will be seasons when what seemed clear becomes uncertain again. Prayer may become quiet, old fears may return, and the God who once seemed near may feel hidden. But hiddenness is not the same as absence.

Trust what has been planted beneath the soil.

You do not need to force yourself to grow. Tend the earth, receive the water, turn toward the light, and allow love to move through you. The Gardener understands seasons better than we do.

And when you forget, begin again.

Receive the bread again. Enter the water again, allow yourself to be anointed again, and return to the light as many times as necessary. Resurrection is not a lesson we master once; it is a life we keep learning to receive.

Most of all, love.

Let whatever knowledge you have gained make you gentler rather than more certain of your superiority. Let freedom make you generous, and let contemplation make you more available to the person standing in front of you. If the hidden life of God is becoming visible anywhere, perhaps it will look something like that.

The ancient words can remain here.

You can carry their life with you.

Go into the ordinary world and allow it to become transparent. Look for the pearl, tend the field, share the bread, forgive when you can, rest when you need to, and keep becoming like the Love upon which you have set your attention.

May you receive resurrection while you are living.

May what has been divided within you find its way toward wholeness. May the name you carry become a gift rather than a burden, and may the Father become less a distant idea and more a Presence discovered within and around you.

May you be washed in the water.

May you be anointed with the oil.

May you be clothed in the light.

And when night comes, may you remember that somewhere beyond what you can presently see, the day has never stopped shining.
